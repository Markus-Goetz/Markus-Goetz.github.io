Markus Götz
======
Assistant Professor for Scalable and Efficient Machine Learning, TUM Campus Heilbronn

Markus Götz is an Assistant Professor for Scalable and Efficient Machine Learning at the [Technical University of Munich (TUM)](https://www.tum.de/en/), Campus Heilbronn. He also continues to lead a research and consulting group as Group Leader for [Helmholtz AI Energy](https://www.helmholtz.ai/) at the [Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT)](https://www.kit.edu/). He received his PhD in Computational Engineering from the [University of Iceland](https://english.hi.is/), carried out in close collaboration with the [Jülich Supercomputing Centre (JSC)](https://www.fz-juelich.de/en/ias/jsc), with a thesis on scalable data analytics in high-performance computing. He specializes in distributed and parallel machine learning on supercomputers, covering topics from scalable clustering and classification to large-scale neural network training. He also has a long track record of research software development: he is lead developer of the [HeAT (Helmholtz Analytics Toolkit)](https://github.com/helmholtz-analytics/heat) distributed tensor framework, and a core contributor to [Propulate](https://github.com/Helmholtz-AI-Energy/propulate), an asynchronous evolutionary optimizer, and [perun](https://github.com/Helmholtz-AI-Energy/perun), a tool for measuring the energy consumption of parallel applications. Markus Götz serves as Helmholtz AI Coordinator Karlsruhe and AI Spokesperson for the Helmholtz Open Science Office, and is a Principal Investigator on numerous national and EU-funded projects.

Research
======
My research focus is on scalable machine learning and data analytics for high-performance computing environments. In particular, I am interested in distributed and parallel training of neural networks, resource-efficient and low-rank model architectures, energy-aware AI, and scalable clustering and classification methods for scientific data. My work spans applications in renewable energy systems, weather modeling, and biomolecular simulation. I care deeply about sustainable and reproducible research software, and much of my work is complemented by open-source tool development aimed at making distributed AI methods accessible and energy-efficient for the wider scientific community.
